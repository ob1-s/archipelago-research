# Organization, continuity, routines, and memory

## What makes the construct difficult

No reviewed literature supports treating “information that survives member turnover” as sufficient for organization. Organization theories emphasize different bundles:

- decided elements such as membership, hierarchy, rules, monitoring, and sanctions;
- communicative flows that constitute membership, self-structure, coordinate activity, and position the organization externally;
- routines as interdependent action patterns with ostensive and performative aspects;
- memory as acquisition, retention, and retrieval distributed across people, structures, culture, transformation processes, and external archives;
- identity claims and commitments that can change while sustaining a recognized trajectory.

These accounts disagree about necessary and sufficient conditions. That disagreement is a reason to decompose H1, not to select whichever definition makes a result easiest.

## Can an organization survive complete membership turnover?

Organizational theory makes the proposition coherent but does not make it automatic. Records, routines, roles, authority, legal identity, assets, boundaries, and relations can outlast particular members. Carley's turnover model shows how organizational knowledge can persist in structure and interpersonal/advisory relations under turnover. It is a model of organizational learning, not direct evidence that any system survives 100% turnover.

At complete turnover, continuity becomes an empirical and institutional achievement. A successor can inherit assets and records yet fail to reproduce practices; perform similar routines yet lack the prior organization's authority; or continue a legal identity while transforming its membership and goals. The relevant question is which relations and commitments are causally reconstructed and recognized, not whether a label or file remains.

## Decomposition of provisional H1

| Component | Positive evidence | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Carrier continuity | artifact/state remains accessible | use, meaning, organization |
| Functional reuse | fresh recipients use state on held-out tasks | endogenous generation or social transmission |
| Endogenous production | agents generate, select, repair, or compress state | organization by itself |
| Transmission | identifiable parent/population pathway affects successor | convention, identity, reproduction |
| Routine persistence | interdependent multi-actor action pattern recurs and adapts | authority, boundary, identity |
| Convention | alternative equilibria depend on expectations/network history | normative obligation or organization |
| Role continuity | differentiated role functions reappear after turnover | recognized authority or membership |
| Authority continuity | decisions are recognized, ratified, enforced, or sanctioned | identity or sovereignty |
| Identity continuity | self-defining claims/boundaries/commitments affect counterfactual behavior | unchanged structure or goals |
| Organizational reconstruction | an integrated bundle returns after disruption through causal lineage | successor reproduction unless parentage/offspring exists |
| Reproduction | parent units produce distinguishable successors with transmitted traits | continuity of the same organization |

## H1 ladder

- **L0 — turnover validity:** all active inference/process state is replaced; shared caches, hidden prompts, weights, and harness memory are audited.
- **L1 — carrier:** state survives.
- **L2 — functional reuse:** fresh agents use it on novel work.
- **L3 — endogenous state:** agents generate or materially transform it.
- **L4 — causal transmission/recovery:** parentage, deletion, and restoration interventions establish inheritance.
- **L5 — routine reconstruction:** a multi-actor procedure survives turnover and perturbation.
- **L6 — convention/norm:** alternative equilibria, expectations, payoff/network sensitivity, or sanctions are demonstrated.
- **L7 — organization-defining structure:** membership/boundary, roles/division of labor, authority/succession, maintenance, or identity-bearing commitments persist under conflict.
- **L8 — organization-like continuity:** multiple L5–L7 components form a causally integrated, self-maintained bundle.
- **L9 — organizational reproduction:** multiple independent units show parentage, transmitted variation, and differential successor production/survival.

The minimum result worth pursuing is L5, **agent-generated cross-turnover routine reconstruction**. L2 should be reported as cross-turnover functional state persistence. The phrase “organizational continuity” is reserved for L8.

## Strong falsification pattern

Strong H1 is undermined if:

- final frozen-state replay matches the entire chain on held-out outcomes;
- researcher-authored, random, or no-state controls match agent-authored state;
- state deletion does not matter;
- state swaps and parentage shuffles do not move behavior;
- isolated populations converge without transmission;
- a fixed prompt, protocol, orchestrator, or common model prior reproduces the effect;
- role, authority, boundary, and succession perturbations are causally inert.

Terminal replay requires a precise interpretation. If a byte-identical final artifact is sufficient for downstream recipients, history is not additionally required for recipient response. The chain may still have been necessary upstream to generate or improve that artifact. Therefore replay can narrow the claim without erasing endogenous production.

## Construct validity requirements

Use convergent and discriminant evidence. A single performance score cannot distinguish memory, routine, convention, authority, or identity. Each claimed construct needs an intervention that should affect it and a nearby construct it should not affect. Examples:

- state swap identifies carrier effects; lineage-by-state interaction tests lineage specificity;
- role permutation tests whether labels or functional dependencies matter;
- contested succession tests recognized authority rather than compliance;
- network/payoff changes test convention dynamics rather than frequency-independent preferences;
- deletion with endogenous repair tests maintenance rather than static storage;
- model-family and encoding swaps test whether shared priors explain independent convergence.

## Judgment

**YES WITH REPAIRS.** H1 is ready for apparatus design because its rungs, controls, and allowed claims can be specified. It is not ready to use “organization” as the default label for any positive downstream state effect.
