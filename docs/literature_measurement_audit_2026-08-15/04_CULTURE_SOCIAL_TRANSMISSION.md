# Culture, social transmission, conventions, and cumulative change

## Required separations

The cultural-evolution literature supplies designs and falsifiers, not a shortcut from shared text to “culture.”

| Claim | Minimum evidence | Nearby false positive |
|---|---|---|
| Content effect | recipient changes after exposure | generic instruction following |
| Social transmission | identifiable source-to-recipient causal pathway | researcher injects final artifact |
| Iterated transmission | state passes through multiple generations and transformation is measured | multiple recipients independently read one frozen text |
| Convention | one of several coordination equilibria becomes population-dependent | shared model prior or dominant answer |
| Norm | behavior includes prescriptive expectation, enforcement, or sanction | frequent behavior without obligation |
| Cumulative culture | socially transmitted improvement or complexity is retained across generations | repeated fidelity with no accumulation |
| Organizational continuity | a bounded, role/authority-bearing collective is reconstructed | any of the above alone |

Mesoudi and Whiten distinguish transmission chains, replacement designs, and closed groups. Caldwell and Millen's replacement microsocieties show cumulative improvement can be studied while participants are replaced. Lewis and Laland show why fidelity matters for accumulation, while reconstruction-based accounts caution that continuity need not be literal copying. Centola and Baronchelli show spontaneous convention in a networked coordination game with local interactions and no global coordination signal.

## One-shot versus iterated effects

Let `S*` be the final state produced by a history `H`.

- **History condition:** the recipient participates in the actual lineage reaching `S*`.
- **Terminal replay:** an otherwise identical fresh recipient receives only byte-identical `S*`.

If outcomes match, `S*` is sufficient for downstream response. The history adds no recipient-side effect. This does not show that history was unnecessary to produce, refine, or select `S*`. The correct conclusion may be “iteratively agent-produced artifact with one-shot functional reuse.”

To claim transformation through transmission, estimate generation-indexed change, path dependence, source-recipient dependence, and loss or gain under bottlenecks. To claim accumulation, use tasks with a measurable performance/complexity frontier and show retention beyond what isolated individuals or a direct researcher seed achieve.

## Conventions and norms

Common behavior is not enough. A convention test needs multiple viable equilibria, interaction-dependent expectations, and sensitivity to network history or local frequency. Randomized payoff, partner, network, and critical-mass interventions distinguish coordination dynamics from a model's static preference.

A norm is a stronger and different construct. It should predict what agents say ought to happen and/or enforcement when violation is individually tempting. Sanction, monitoring, or exclusion interventions are needed. A convention can exist without moral force; a written rule can exist without being enacted.

## LLM evidence

Acerbi and Stubbersfield placed GPT-3 responses in transmission-chain-like summarization experiments and found content biases analogous to prior human results. This demonstrates patterned transformation in an engineered chain, not a self-constituting population.

Ashery, Aiello, and Baronchelli placed LLM calls in pairwise naming games. Local interaction histories were supplied by the harness; populations converged on conventions and could exhibit collective bias without a global coordinating signal. This is the closest directly relevant published design reviewed. It still does not establish Archipelago H1:

- an invocation is not shown to be a persistent member;
- the harness chooses partners, writes and supplies memory, and implements payoffs;
- there is no complete-population turnover intervention;
- roles, authority, membership, maintenance, and organizational identity are absent;
- public criticism argues that training-data leakage can be observationally equivalent to emergence in some games; the authors dispute that interpretation, so contamination-resistant novel games remain important.

Thus the allowed claim is “emergent convention under an engineered LLM-population protocol,” not culture-bearing organization.

## Controls for Archipelago

- no-state and random-state controls;
- researcher-authored content matched for information;
- terminal replay of the same final artifact;
- parentage shuffle and partner-network rewiring;
- state-by-lineage factorial;
- isolated rediscovery lineages;
- novel symbols/tasks generated after model training or procedurally randomized;
- generation-direction reversal and order controls;
- task-performance baselines for isolated individuals and persistent non-turnover groups;
- model-family and encoding swaps;
- explicit sanction/payoff interventions for norms/conventions;
- complete audit of harness-created memory and global signals.

## Allowed language

- Positive recipient contrast: **content-mediated recipient effect**.
- Multi-generation causal pathway: **iterated social/information transmission**.
- Alternative equilibria with population dependence: **convention formation/persistence**.
- Retained improvement across replacement: **cumulative cultural transmission**.
- Use **culture** only as an umbrella after multiple transmitted practices or representations show population-level maintenance and transformation; do not use it to imply organization.

Legacy repository/package names such as `culture_v1` are historical implementation labels, not validated construct claims. Future reports should translate them into the measured recipient, policy-cue, transmission, or convention effect.
