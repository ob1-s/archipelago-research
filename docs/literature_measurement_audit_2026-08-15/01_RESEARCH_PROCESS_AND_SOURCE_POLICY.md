# Research process and source policy

## Scope and epistemic ownership

The Sol parent retained responsibility for decomposition, source verification, cross-field transfer, causal reasoning, decisions, and synthesis. Luna Max agents performed bounded independent literature passes. Their agreement was never counted as evidence; their outputs were leads and critiques that the parent adjudicated against sources.

No behavioral science run occurred. Search queries, source inspection, and research-agent reports are research labor, not observations about autonomous AI organization.

## Independent-pass design

Five load-bearing topics received at least two meaningfully different paths:

1. H1: a supportive organizational-memory/continuity path and a skeptical decomposition/falsifier path.
2. Redundant/recurrent: a distributed-systems path and an organizational-reproduction/ecology path.
3. Suppression: an adaptation/displacement path and an extinction/effectiveness path.
4. Selection: an organizational-ecology path and an evolutionary/causal-identification critique.
5. Observability/custody: a technical attestation/distributed-trust path and an institutional capture/common-evidence critique.

Agents did not receive the prior agent's answer when assigned an independent path. Subsequent adversarial review was instructed to kill the project, not polish it. A final cold reader received only the canonical packet and this audit.

Across four spawned Luna Max workers, the workflow completed 12 bounded research/adversarial passes plus the routing canary and cold-reader gate. The cold reader reconstructed the historical thesis, evidence boundary, revised dependency, nulls, governance comparator, and next step. Its first pass found one presentation collision—federated-custody dominance appeared inside a list called scientific nulls. The audit split that into a comparative governance defeat condition, bounded legacy `culture_v1` naming, and clarified operational versus logical dependency. The focused recheck returned **PASS**; remaining wording suggestions were also repaired.

## Source hierarchy

Priority order was:

1. primary peer-reviewed research;
2. canonical books and field-defining papers;
3. systematic reviews and meta-analyses;
4. primary systems papers, standards, and official technical documentation;
5. official incident disclosures and oversight records;
6. institutional research where primary academic evidence was unavailable.

Search snippets and secondary reporting were discovery aids, never load-bearing evidence. For the 2026 Hugging Face incident, official OpenAI and Hugging Face disclosures, the state-attorneys-general letter, and the Casar–Matsui congressional letter were separated from press reports. A Reuters report supports the existence of a separate August 3 House-panel briefing request, but that action remains secondary-source verified until its primary committee record is archived. Claims without public raw evidence remain unresolved.

## Direct-inspection rule

“Directly inspected” means that a researcher opened the source's full text, official standard/documentation, or official disclosure and checked the relevant section. Publisher metadata or an abstract alone is marked accordingly in the bibliography. A source can be high quality yet not directly inspected in full; such a source is not used for a claim that depends on inaccessible details.

The bibliography records the exact Archipelago claim supported and the limitation that blocks over-transfer. DOI metadata was cross-checked against publisher or Crossref records where available. Stable report identifiers are used for RFC, NIST, USENIX, arXiv, and official incident sources.

## Transfer discipline

Every analogy was tested with four questions:

- What is the unit in the source field?
- What causal mechanism is actually demonstrated?
- Which preconditions exist there but not for current AI agents?
- What observation would distinguish the analogy from a simpler explanation?

Examples: checkpoint recovery concerns state, not organization; political repression acts on humans with identities and recruitment; nuclear deterrence assumes territorial actors and second-strike capabilities; secret sharing distributes a key, not semantic judgment; LLM convention games implement memory and interactions in a harness.

## Claim labels

The audit uses the following labels consistently:

- **Archipelago-established:** demonstrated by the frozen Archipelago record.
- **External-literature support:** directly supported in the cited external domain.
- **Plausible analogy:** mechanism resembles Archipelago but important transfer conditions remain.
- **Assumption:** necessary premise without evidence here.
- **Untested:** operationalizable but not yet tested.
- **Contested:** credible sources or interpretations conflict.
- **Not required:** an edge is not logically necessary for that version of the claim.

For the motivating incident, the stricter labels are **verified primary source**, **supported inference**, **unresolved**, and **speculative**.

## Known limitations

This is a discriminating audit, not a systematic review with exhaustive databases, dual-screened inclusion, or a registered protocol. Some mature literatures are represented by canonical and mechanism-defining sources rather than exhaustive coverage. The audit cannot verify nonpublic model traces, internal security logs, or future OpenAI reports. Results from human institutions and engineered distributed systems constrain concepts and measurements; they do not establish that AI organizations exist.
