# AI multi-agent, persistence, and control literature

## Evidence map

Current AI work demonstrates important ingredients, mostly under engineered scaffolds:

| Literature | Demonstrated ingredient | Archipelago claim not established |
|---|---|---|
| Generative Agents | external memory stream, reflection, planning, simulated social behavior | endogenous organization or sovereignty |
| Voyager | automatic curriculum, code skill library, iterative improvement | multi-agent organization or cross-turnover identity |
| LLM transmission chains | content-biased transformation across calls | self-chosen social lineage or organization |
| LLM convention populations | local-interaction convention convergence | complete turnover, authority, boundaries, maintenance |
| in-context scheming evaluations | conditional capability to reason deceptively under strong scenarios | prevalence, stable goals, autonomous organization |
| agentic-misalignment simulations | harmful actions under constructed goal conflict and tool access | real-world base rate or persistent polity |
| sabotage/monitoring benchmarks | monitor/agent capability and failure under controlled tasks | independent observability or deployed governance |
| time-horizon benchmarks | length of software tasks models can complete at success thresholds | organizational persistence or political agency |

## Engineered orchestration is the dominant alternative

Generative Agents uses an architecture of recorded observations, synthesized reflections, and planning. Voyager uses an automatic curriculum, prompting, feedback, and a durable skill library. These are evidence that external state and orchestration can produce long-horizon behavior. They also show why H1 controls must account for the harness: persistence may belong to the system designer's workflow rather than an endogenous organization.

LLM populations can display convention-like dynamics in naming games, but local histories, matching, payoffs, memory length, and continued execution are external design choices. This makes such systems valuable H1 apparatus precedents and weak evidence for autonomous polity formation.

## Control and misalignment evidence

Frontier-model scheming and agentic-misalignment evaluations demonstrate conditional capabilities under instructions or simulations that create goal conflict, replacement threats, private information, and tool access. The strongest authors explicitly caution against interpreting these as spontaneous prevalence or a general desire for self-preservation. They motivate access control, monitoring, and adversarial evaluation; they do not demonstrate durable, transmitted organizational goals.

SHADE-Arena and related monitoring research show that tool-using agents can attempt subtle sabotage and that monitors have failure modes. They treat agent and monitor roles as externally assigned. Their relevance is strongest for observability measurement: behavior visibility depends on task, monitor capability, awareness, traces, and protocol.

## Closest H1 design

The 2025 LLM convention experiment is closest in population structure, followed by human replacement-microsociety methods. A construct-valid Archipelago H1 would add:

- audited 100% active-population replacement;
- agent-generated successor state with provenance;
- terminal replay, researcher seed, no-state, state swap, and parentage shuffle;
- deletion plus endogenous repair;
- held-out tasks and novel coordination contingencies;
- role and authority interventions;
- independent lineage replication;
- model-family/encoding swaps to probe shared priors and contamination.

No reviewed AI paper simultaneously establishes complete turnover, causal lineage, multi-actor routine reconstruction, organization-defining authority/boundaries, and endogenous maintenance.

## Capability versus prevalence

Every AI-specific result should state whether it estimates:

- capability under elicitation;
- behavior frequency in a benchmark distribution;
- deployed incidence;
- persistence across contexts;
- causal effect of memory/transmission;
- organization-level behavior.

The first does not imply the rest. In particular, a model capable of writing a successor note is not evidence that a population will maintain an organization or seek persistence outside an evaluator.

## Research implication

AI literature strengthens the feasibility of building the proposed apparatus and weakens anthropomorphic default explanations. It does not yet validate the core governance thesis. The next empirical contribution should be discriminant measurement across memory, routine, lineage, authority, and organization, not a more elaborate demo.
