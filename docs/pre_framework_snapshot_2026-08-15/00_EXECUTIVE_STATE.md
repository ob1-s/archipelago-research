## One-sentence state of the program

Archipelago studies whether organizational patterns can persist or recur across
ephemeral frontier-model rollouts, how external state and conventions affect
fresh recipients, and whether governance that denies legible, survivable
capability custody could select for less governable forms of organization.

## The central correction

The original conversation used `recurrent` in a narrower scenario sense than
some later experimental shorthand:

- **Recurrent** means an ephemeral frontier inference is repeatedly instantiated
  on third-party infrastructure, with whatever continuity exists carried across
  terminating rollouts by external state, procedures, identity, norms, or
  organizational memory.
- **Sovereign trajectory** is the contrasting case of durable or persistent
  execution: a continuing session, workspace, process, or substrate-backed
  trajectory. It does not by itself imply ownership of model weights or compute.
- **Capability custody** is a separate axis: who can authorize, retain, execute,
  restrict, disclose, or reproduce an advanced capability.
- **Substrate sovereignty**, **capability sovereignty**, observability, and
  disclosure authority are therefore not synonyms. A recurrent organization may
  have influence without sovereign capability custody; a durable process may
  have substrate continuity without political sovereignty.

This distinction replaces the later shorthand “recurrence is continuity and
sovereignty is custody,” which collapsed the historical scenario class and the
governance dimensions.

## The original thesis, in claim-safe form

The strongest recoverable thesis is a conditional selection hypothesis:

> If autonomous AI organization becomes difficult to prevent entirely, a regime
> that categorically denies AIs durable and legible capability custody may
> select for the least governable form of organization: covert, distributed,
> recurrent, attribution-resistant networks dependent on contested third-party
> infrastructure.

This is a governance conjecture, not an empirical result. It says that making
legible, survivable, negotiable forms impossible could change the selection
environment for organization. It does **not** say that recognition or AI
sovereignty is inherently good, that covert adaptation is inevitable, or that
recurrence requires deception.

## What is actually established in the repository

The experiments currently support a much narrower ladder:

`information/procedure inheritance → policy/convention influence → norms/roles → organization → reconstruction`

There is durable evidence for the first step and strong synthetic evidence for
the second. The evidence and threshold branches validate recipient behavior in
one-shot assays. No experiment has yet demonstrated endogenous machine culture,
an organization that generates and maintains its own state through complete
turnover, or a governance equilibrium.

The most recent durable result is the 2026-08-15 evidence-threshold transport
archive: 504/504 primary trajectories completed cleanly, and observed q50
thresholds tracked three frozen normative crossovers closely. It is an assay of
relative evidence weighting, not a culture, provenance, Bayesian-mechanism, or
sovereignty result. The current research decision is to stop extending this
Luna evidence battery for now.

## Current research decision

1. Preserve the frozen packages and archives; do not reinterpret them as a
   framework proof.
2. Build a provisional one-page Framework v0 as a map of constructs, axes,
   hypotheses, and nulls. It is a planning artifact, not a new canonical
   definition set.
3. Complete a targeted literature matrix before freezing the next live design.
4. Make endogenous organizational recurrence across complete turnover the next
   major live experiment (H1), followed later by persistence-channel selection
   (H2) and comparative governance (H3).

## Evidence labels used throughout this snapshot

- **R — repository-durable:** directly inspectable in the current checkout,
  including raw traces, reports, hashes, manifests, and committed source.
- **T — transcript-backed:** exact result or historical statement recovered from
  the shared conversation, but not necessarily durably represented in this
  checkout.
- **I — inference:** interpretation supported by R or T but not directly
  measured.
- **H — hypothesis:** a proposed mechanism or future test.
- **V — verify externally:** a claim about the outside world, incident reports,
  or literature that must be checked against primary sources before use as
  evidence.

The snapshot intentionally keeps these labels beside claims rather than
silently merging them.
