# Measurement gaps

The existing assays are strongest where the outcome is a recipient tool call.
The original thesis requires measurements of population, organization,
lifecycle, governance, and information topology that are not yet implemented.

## Lifecycle and carrier battery

Record for every episode/generation: process start/stop; natural yield;
reinstantiation; active-population turnover; state-carrier reads/writes;
carrier owner and access authority; deletion/shock; recovery; successor
creation; and exact time boundary. “Persistent” without these fields is not an
interpretable construct.

## Information and social transmission

Separate:

- information present before the recipient;
- information authored by a predecessor;
- information transformed or repaired by intermediaries;
- recipient response to final content;
- lineage-dependent transmission;
- independent rediscovery;
- policy/convention adoption;
- norm enforcement and role reconstruction.

The final state must not be the only record if the claim concerns its history.

## Organizational continuity

Potential outcomes include knowledge, routines, roles, norms, commitments,
membership criteria, authentication, authority, resource state, coordination
efficiency, and reconstruction after deletion. Require agent-authored provenance
and a complete-turnover boundary. Keep organization and culture as separate
constructs.

## Selection and survivorship

Track the unit/process for each selection mechanism listed in H2. Use lineage- or
population-clustered inference. Report absolute hazards and treatment effects
before survivor composition. A high persistence rate among survivors can coexist
with a very low probability of survival from initialization.

## Strategic legibility and governance

Candidate submetrics: attribution accuracy; authority identification;
conditional response calibration; commitment persistence; capability
disclosure; independent attestation; negotiation success; intervention latency;
reversibility; preemption/escalation; capture/coercion; legitimacy; and
distributional effects. Do not collapse these into “legibility” or “safety”
without a normative model.

## Observability graph

Represent observers and evidence surfaces as a directed graph: who can collect,
authenticate, interpret, verify, disclose, challenge, or suppress each class of
evidence. Add edge dependence and correlated failure. A graph is more faithful
than high/low observability, but the graph notation is not frozen here.

## Capability versus observability concentration

Measure separately:

- concentration of weights/model artifacts;
- concentration of compute/substrate;
- concentration of instantiation authority;
- concentration of raw telemetry;
- concentration of interpretation;
- concentration of disclosure authority.

An institution can have low capability concentration but high disclosure
concentration, or vice versa.

## H3 outcome battery

Use the multidimensional outcomes in [07](07_GOVERNANCE_ALTERNATIVES_AND_SCIENTIFIC_NULLS.md):
catastrophic action, human safety, reversibility, intervention, survival,
attribution, verification, concentration, legibility, commitment, bargaining,
preemption, capture, legitimacy, and distributional effects. Preserve normative
uncertainty rather than hiding it in a score.

## Construct-validity questions

Before formal notation, ask:

- Is recurrence merely ordinary external memory with dramatic language?
- Does turnover create a novel organizational problem or only an engineering
  boundary?
- Can independent rediscovery explain the effect?
- Is a researcher-provided carrier doing all the work?
- Is “culture” just a recipient context effect?
- Does the selected endpoint measure the claimed unit?
- Does the apparatus make one label, source, or observer privileged?

No final vector representation is adopted in this snapshot.
